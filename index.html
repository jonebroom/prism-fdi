User Manual

PRISM ISM Database · Version 1.0 · Data coverage: 2007–2023
1. Platform Overview

PRISM (Policy Review and Investment Screening Mechanisms) is an interactive data platform providing comprehensive time-series data on FDI screening mechanisms across 38 countries from 2007 to 2023.

Countries38 (OECD · EU/EEA · Five Eyes)
Coverage2007 – 2023 (annual observations)
Records650 time-series · 143 laws · 230 changes · 141 policy texts
Sectors tracked36 specific + 8 aggregate categories
Indicators13 binary procedural powers + 4 quantitative fields

Intended users: legal practitioners & compliance teams, investment professionals, policy researchers, and government officials benchmarking against peers.

3. Tab-by-Tab Guide
01 Overview
The world map shades countries by your chosen dimension (strictness score, sectors covered, review threshold, or max review days). Click any country to see a summary card; click View Profile → to open the full Country tab. The trend line below tracks how many countries had a given feature active each year — click any year on the chart to jump the global slider to that year.
02 Country
Practitioner summary card at the top answers "do I need to file?" — showing filing requirement, review authority, threshold, and max review days. Below: a radar chart (8 dimensions vs. OECD average + a comparison year), full field list, 16 binary indicators, and a regulation timeline. Click any regulation bubble to open its details drawer with policy text and AI summary link.
03 Compare
Parallel-coordinates chart with one colored line per country. Drag on any axis to filter — only lines passing through the selected range stay highlighted. Click any line to navigate to that country's profile. Use the color toggle to switch between group colors and strictness gradient.
04 Evolution
Five charts: coverage-type pie, notification × pre-approval bubble matrix, stacked area over time, strictness band chart (average ± min/max), and scatter plot (threshold or timeframe vs. strictness). The regulation network graph shows replacement relationships — click any node to open that country's profile.
05 Changes
Bar chart of annual global legislative activity broken down by type (New Law · Amendment · Executive Order · Implementing Regulation). Click any bar to select that year — the event list below updates and the global year slider syncs. Click any event card to read the policy text.
06 Sectors
Heatmap rows = sectors, columns = countries. Toggle between 36 specific sectors and 8 aggregate categories (with % coverage shading). The ranking bar chart shows how many countries cover each sector — click ▶ Play to animate the ranking over time.

5. AI Data Assistant

Supports natural-language queries against PRISM data. The assistant is context-aware — it knows the current year, selected country, active group filters, and active tab.

What it can do
Compare mechanisms across countries · identify trends · explain data fields · list countries with specific features · summarize individual regulations (via Summarize with AI → in any policy drawer)
Configuration (⚙ icon)
Choose provider (Anthropic/OpenAI/DeepSeek/MiniMax/GLM/Kimi/custom), model, and API key. Keys are stored only in your browser's local storage and sent directly to the provider — never routed elsewhere.

Press Enter to send · Shift+Enter to add a new line. Responses are capped at ~250 words.

7. Key Concepts
Strictness score (0–13)
Counts how many of 13 procedural powers exist in law. Measures procedural strength, not likelihood of blocking a deal — enforcement culture and political context also matter.
Coverage type
Cross-sectoralAll industries — any foreign deal may be reviewed
SectoralSpecific industries only (e.g. defense, energy)
MixedBroad screen plus sector-specific add-ons
Asset-basedTriggered by asset type (land, infrastructure), not sector
Draft / Not yet in forceLaw proposed or passed but not yet effective
Notification vs. pre-approval
Notification: investor must inform the government — deal can often proceed during review. Pre-approval: government clearance required before closing. Pre-approval is the stricter requirement.
The 13 strictness indicators
Formal Call-inGov. can open review without a filing
Ownership ReviewTriggered on incremental stake increases
Filing FeesFee required to submit a filing
MitigationApproval with binding conditions (e.g. asset divestiture)
FinesPenalties for completing a deal without approval
National Security TestNat. security explicitly named as grounds
Net Benefit TestEconomic benefit assessed (common in Canada)
Competition TestMarket competition also considered
Interagency ReviewMultiple agencies formally involved (e.g. CFIUS)
Tiered AuthorityDifferent approval levels by deal size or sector
Local RepresentationLocal directors/legal rep required post-close
Enhanced Gov. ControlGolden share retained in strategic companies
Co-locationSensitive data/infrastructure must stay in-country
8. Common Questions
Does this tell me definitively whether my deal needs approval?
No — PRISM gives you a well-researched starting point, not legal advice. Rules change, thresholds vary by sector, and many countries have exceptions not captured here. Always verify with the relevant government authority or legal counsel before proceeding.
A country shows no mechanism — does that mean my deal is free to proceed?
It means no formal FDI screening mechanism was recorded for that country and year. Other rules (antitrust, sector-specific regulations, general company law) may still apply. The absence of a screening mechanism is not a green light.
The strictness score looks low for a country I know is strict — why?
The score only measures whether specific procedural features exist in law — not how aggressively they are used in practice. A country with a score of 4 might block deals more frequently than one with a score of 9, depending on enforcement culture and political context.
Why does the year filter affect some charts but not others?
The year slider changes the "snapshot year" for cross-sectional views (map, country profiles, sector heatmap). Charts that show trends over time always show the full 2007–2023 range — the selected year is marked as a reference point only.
How current is the data?
The database is complete through end of 2023. Laws enacted or amended in 2024 onward are not yet reflected. Always check official sources for the most recent rules.
Can I cite or download this data?
Please cite the PRISM ISM Database and contact the research team for the working paper reference and data access.
