PRISM ISM Database · Reference & Notes
Data Sources

The PRISM ISM (Investment Screening Mechanism) Database covers 38 countries over 2007–2024. Data is compiled from official government sources, legislation texts, OECD/IMF policy reviews, and the authors' primary research.

Each country-year record captures whether a formal FDI screening mechanism was in place and its key procedural and substantive features.

01 · Overview
Global FDI Screening Mechanism Distribution
World choropleth map. Country shading reflects the selected dimension (strictness score, sectors covered, review threshold, or timeframe) in the chosen year. Darker fill = higher value. Hover for a tooltip with the country's value; click to jump to the Country tab for detailed analysis. Use the dimension selector above the map to switch metrics.
Global Trend
Dual-axis area + bar chart spanning 2007–2024. The shaded area (left axis) tracks how many countries in the filtered set have each selected feature active that year. The bar layer (right axis) shows the count of entirely new mechanisms enacted globally per year. Two vertical dashed lines mark 2019 (pre-pandemic) and 2020 (post-pandemic onset) as structural reference points.
Annual Snapshot
Ranked bar chart of all currently filtered countries sorted by the selected dimension for the active year. Each bar is colored by the country's coverage type (Sectoral / Cross-sectoral / Mixed / Asset-based). Provides a quick cross-country ranking at a glance without needing to open individual country pages.
02 · Country
Multi-Dimensional Radar
Spider / radar chart with 8 axes corresponding to the core procedural dimensions (NS Test, Formal Call-in, Fines, Mitigation, Interagency Review, Net Benefit Test, Enhanced Gov. Control, Ownership Review). Each axis is binary (0 or 1). Up to three series are plotted simultaneously: the selected country in the current year, the OECD average for that year, and optionally the same country in a comparison year (choose via the "Compare year" dropdown).
Key Indicators
KPI card panel showing the country's headline metrics for the selected year: strictness score (out of 13), number of sectors covered, equity review threshold (%), review timeframe (days), and coverage type. Values are extracted directly from the country-year record.
Procedural Requirements & Coverage
Checklist grid of all 13 strictness indicators plus supplementary coverage fields (greenfield, real estate, export control / dual-use). Each cell shows ✓ (present) or ✗ (absent) for the selected year. Allows a complete at-a-glance audit of which powers the mechanism holds.
Regulation Timeline
Horizontal bubble timeline of all regulations recorded for the country, ordered chronologically. Bubble size encodes the number of sectors the regulation covers; bubble color encodes coverage type. Hover for the regulation title and date; click to open a side drawer with the full regulation metadata and policy text excerpt (where available).
03 · Comparison
Multi-Country Parallel Coordinates
Parallel coordinates plot showing all currently filtered countries in the active year. Each vertical axis represents one procedural or coverage dimension; each polyline is a country. Drag a range on any axis to filter displayed lines. Hover a line to highlight it and see the country label. Useful for identifying clusters of similar mechanisms and outliers. Countries are colored by group (OECD / EU / Five Eyes).
04 · Evolution
Coverage Type Distribution
Donut / pie chart for the active year showing the share of countries by coverage type (Sectoral, Cross-sectoral, Mixed, Asset-based, Draft, Passed). Click a segment to filter the other charts on the tab to that subset and see which countries belong to the selected type.
Notification × Pre-approval Matrix
Bubble matrix where the X-axis is "notification requirement present" (yes/no) and the Y-axis is "pre-approval requirement present" (yes/no), creating four quadrants. Each bubble represents a cluster of countries sharing that combination; bubble size encodes the number of countries. Reveals the dominant procedural model for the selected year.
Coverage Type Evolution
Stacked area chart running 2007–2024. Each band corresponds to a coverage type; height shows how many countries used that model each year. Read vertically to see the composition in any year; read horizontally to observe the structural shift — most visibly the rise of cross-sectoral mechanisms after 2019.
Strictness Distribution Over Time
Line + range chart showing the mean strictness score (0–13) across filtered countries per year, with a shaded band from the 25th to 75th percentile. Tracks the overall tightening trend and the spread (heterogeneity) of strictness levels across countries.
Threshold vs Strictness
Scatter plot for the active year. X-axis: equity ownership review threshold (%); Y-axis: strictness score (0–13). Each point is a country, colored by coverage type. Reveals whether countries with lower thresholds (more sensitive to minority stakes) also tend to score higher on procedural strictness. Click a point to navigate to that country's detail page.
Regulation Supersession Network
Force-directed graph of regulatory succession. Each node is a piece of legislation; directed edges point from superseded laws to their replacements. Node color encodes country group; node size encodes the number of sectors covered. Click a node to open the Country tab for that country. Reveals reform chains and legislative lineage across the dataset.

Global Legislative Activity
Grouped bar chart counting new or amended mechanisms per year, segmented by event type (new enactment, amendment, expansion, etc.). Click any bar or bar segment to filter the event list below to that year and type. The 2019–2021 spike is clearly visible, reflecting the wave of reform triggered by heightened national security concerns.

Chronological list of all legislative events matching the year/type selected in the bar chart above (or all events if no filter is active). 06 · Sectors
Sector Coverage Heatmap
Matrix heatmap with sectors as rows and countries as columns. A dark cell means that sector is covered by the country's screening mechanism in the selected year; a light cell means it is not. Strictness Score — 13 Indicators

Each country-year is coded on 13 binary indicators (1 = present, 0 = absent). The sum (0–13) is the overall strictness index used in all charts.

Formal Call-inGovernment can proactively open a review without investor filing
Ownership ReviewTriggered by changes in equity ownership thresholds
Filing FeesMandatory fees payable when submitting a notification
MitigationAuthority to impose binding remedies or behavioral conditions
FinesMonetary penalties for non-compliance or attempted evasion
National Security TestExplicit national security standard as the review criterion
Net Benefit TestBroader economic benefit to the host country assessed
Competition TestAntitrust / market competition dimension formally included
Interagency ReviewMultiple government agencies formally participate in review
Tiered AuthorityDifferent approval tiers (e.g. ministry vs. cabinet) by deal size
Local RepresentationRequirement for local board directors or legal representatives
Enhanced Gov. ControlSpecial government shares or veto rights (golden share)
Co-locationData storage or facility co-location requirements imposed

FAQ
Why does a country show score 0 for a given year?
Either no formal screening mechanism existed that year, or it existed but none of the 13 features were coded as present. Check the Country tab Regulation Timeline for the legislative history.
What does "OECD average" refer to on charts?
The unweighted mean across all OECD member countries in the dataset for that year. Only countries with an active mechanism in that year are included in the average.
How does the year filter interact with charts?
The year slider in the left rail controls the "active year" for all cross-sectional charts (map, snapshot, scatter, heatmap). Time-series charts (Global Trend, Strictness Distribution) always show the full 2007–2024 span and use the selected year only as a reference marker.
Can I cite this data?
Please cite the PRISM ISM Database and contact the research team for the working paper reference.
